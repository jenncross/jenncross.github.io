How does median filtering work?

The median filter slides a window across the signal one sample at a time. At each position it sorts the values inside the window and picks the middle value (the median) as the output.

Outlying spikes get replaced by the median of their neighbors, which smooths out high-frequency noise while preserving the shape of the underlying signal. In Python, scipy.signal.medfilt does this.

Try: Set window to 3 (minimal), then increase to 21 and watch peaks change.

How does Gaussian-weighted smoothing work?

Instead of treating all window values equally, Gaussian smoothing applies weights shaped like a bell curve. The center value gets the highest weight and values farther from center contribute less.

In Python, generate weights with scipy.signal.gaussian and apply them with numpy.convolve.

Try: Watch the weight values inside the window as the filter steps through.

What are high-frequency vs. low-frequency signals?

A signal is a composite of components that change at different rates. Low-frequency components change slowly (like the overall heartbeat shape). High-frequency components change rapidly (like noise spikes).

Smoothing filters are low-pass filters: they let low frequencies pass and remove high frequencies. The window size controls the cutoff.

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) describes how much "good" signal you have relative to noise. High SNR = clean. Low SNR = buried in noise.

How does peak detection work?

The algorithm scans left to right. At each point it checks: (1) Is this a local maximum (higher than both neighbors)? (2) Is it above the threshold?

If both are true the point is a candidate. Then: is it at least min_distance samples from the last accepted peak? If yes, accepted (green). If too close, rejected (red).

The period is the spacing between consecutive peaks. A stable average period means a consistent rhythm like a heartbeat.

Why filter before finding peaks?

Noise creates false local maxima. A small random spike can trick the algorithm.

Smoothing first removes small spikes so the real peaks stand out.

Try: Set pre-filter to 1 (none) and watch false peaks appear.

Why does baseline drift break peak detection?

Peak detection uses a fixed threshold. When the baseline drifts upward, noise bumps cross the threshold (false positives). When it drifts downward, real peaks drop below (missed detections).

Real EKG data almost always has baseline wander from breathing, electrode movement, or electrical interference.

How does the derivative act as a high-pass filter?

The derivative dy[i] = y[i+1] - y[i] measures how fast the signal changes. Slow drift changes very little between samples so its derivative is near zero. Sharp QRS spikes change rapidly so they produce large derivative values.

This removes slow drift (low-frequency) and preserves fast spikes (high-frequency). That is exactly what a high-pass filter does.

The smoothing filters in the Filtering tab are low-pass (remove high frequencies). The derivative is the opposite: a high-pass filter that removes low frequencies like baseline drift.

How do zero crossings find periods?

After taking the derivative, each original peak becomes a point where the derivative crosses from positive to negative. This is because the signal goes from rising to falling at a peak.

By finding these zero crossings we locate peaks without needing a threshold. The spacing between consecutive crossings gives the heartbeat period.
